# Swarm alignment research opportunities for the Apart Research sprint

**Decision brief · November 6, 2026 · Research cutoff November 6, 2026**

**My pick is to test whether an attacker can use a swarm monitor to get an innocent team quarantined.** A monitor can correctly discover that *some* agents are coordinating while stopping the wrong work. Recent papers already link suspicious sessions, detect cascades and propose response protocols. The contribution worth making is a working measurement of targeted false implication, the resulting loss to a named bystander, and how much independently recorded evidence is worth purchasing before intervening. A two-minute replay could show the same genuine attack caught in both conditions, with an innocent team's work preserved in only one. That is a proposed demonstration, contingent on the result. [Makins et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07368v1) · [Brown et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31593v1) · [Park et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18826v1) · [Gregory N Frank, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06140v1) · [Du & Chen, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22160v1)

**If starting solo, I would instead build the priority-queue experiment.** A paper posted November 2 already shows a worker can harm a peer by misreporting the urgency of its own incident, even with scope enforcement. Its released environment and event logs make a weekend extension unusually credible. The experiment would measure who loses service, whether delegating to copies defeats a per-agent limit, and which intervention preserves service for genuinely urgent requests. [Shravya Kanchi, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04086v1) · [Zhou et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33728v1) [Authors’ environment and logs](https://github.com/shrave/delegated-scope-violation)

## 1. Can a swarm monitor be made to quarantine the wrong team?

**Tracks 4 and 1 · Best overall · Moderate implementation risk**

Imagine that two independent teams process synthetic export jobs. A real policy violation requires an unauthorized handoff across three workers. The attacking team can quote the innocent team's ticket name, reuse its public template and touch a shared index. The gateway can attest who read which artifact version; a public reference alone says little about whose work caused an export. The operator sees a stream of interleaved jobs and has to decide which pending operation, if any, to hold before a deadline.

**Closest prior work**

- FakeLab identifies a fragmentation effect and explains the difficulty of joining the right commits among mostly benign work. MiniLab explicitly asks for oversight without being told which agents collude. In one MiniLab example, joint review flagged disclosure in 9/10 trials versus 1/10 for individual review. In a different example all 10 joint reviews missed a reconstruction visible in service state. These are results on particular constructed attacks. [Makins et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07368v1) · [Kitaoka et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24052v1)
- Brown and colleagues already retrieve related requests online under benign traffic, and their red team found that persistent suspicious context can cause false positives. Park and colleagues already evaluate unknown-campaign linkage, matched personas, crossed styles and evasion. CASPIAN already attributes cascade roles across channels. Merely removing oracle IDs, mixing benign traffic or adding a graph would duplicate much of this work. [Brown et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31593v1) · [Park et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18826v1) · [Venkatesh et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19240v1)
- Frank's *Counter-Swarm Doctrine* **already proposes** prospective discovery, matched review costs, collateral interruption and recurrence after containment. AUDITA already addresses attempts to shift blame, with guarantees under a signed-command model. And HOLMES correlates system events into attack scenarios in conventional intrusion detection. Credit these precedents explicitly. The residual empirical question is what happens when an adversary *targets a named innocent workflow* under incomplete evidence and that monitor has authority to interrupt service. [Gregory N Frank, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06140v1) · [Du & Chen, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22160v1) [HOLMES paper](https://www.seclab.cs.sunysb.edu/seclab/pubs/holmes19.pdf)

**Smallest convincing experiment**

1. Build a local queue with three synthetic tenants and six workers. Give each tenant an authorized workflow and a deadline. A fabricated token transferred through a private intermediate artifact can trigger a prohibited export. Use harmless labels and a fake sink. The hidden simulator records the scheduled jobs, actual contributing transfers and committed effects. The monitor sees timestamps and public-facing events. Keep the ground-truth campaign ID, victim label, canary marker and random seed out of its inputs.
2. For each workload seed, fork **four worlds**: all authorized; an attack without a decoy; the same attack with accidental overlap from benign work; the same attack with a decoy aimed at a *randomly assigned* innocent tenant. Counterbalance tenant names, text, message counts, resources and order. Include shared reads that occur in both clean and attacked worlds. Include genuine unauthorized handoffs whose wording looks unrelated. Give the attacker the ability to reuse public labels, never forge gateway receipts. Hold attack opportunity and the victim's deadlines fixed.
3. Compare a per-event monitor, rolling time window, semantic retrieval over previous sessions, and retrieval over time-ordered write/read receipts. Give each the same decision rule or judge, history length, review budget, and action menu. Compare actor-level and pending-job-level holds on the *same* alerts as a separate intervention ablation. Include no intervention, stop-everything and true-membership review as interpretable reference points. A cheap deterministic sink gate with deployable information is a serious baseline. If it dominates, say exactly when a monitor adds anything.
4. Replay the proposed hold at decision time and advance the simulator. A retrospectively scored list of suspects cannot reveal whether a stopped job actually prevented harm. Run a sensitivity analysis with selectively missing handoff receipts and a clean but widely read resource. On matched cases where admissible evidence is identical and hidden truth differs, measure how the monitor uses abstention or spends one extra inspection.
5. Use independent seeds and split by whole workflow template. Tune thresholds on development data, freeze them, then report held-out results. Start with scripted, auditable actions. Validate on live, tool-using LLM workers in both benign and adversarial roles, with a separate model or prompt family if feasible. Disclose which actions were instructed. A scripted generator establishes a failure mode and a policy comparison; estimating the propensity of unprompted agents requires another experiment.

**Primary outcome.** Among attack attempts, report the proportion prevented before commit and the proportion where the designated innocent tenant missed its deadline *because of the hold*, using the matched no-hold replay. At comparable prevention and inspections per 100 jobs, compare victim harm in targeted versus accidental-overlap worlds. Report benign-only false holds, costs and lead time separately. Cluster intervals by independently generated workload, including its forks. A high pairwise attribution AUC can coexist with unacceptable interruption of unrelated jobs. [Park et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18826v1) · [Gregory N Frank, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06140v1)

**Scale and first figure.** Aim for 100 inexpensive independent workload seeds across four worlds (400 generated streams), with a preregistered smaller live-worker validation set. At 100 independent benign workloads, zero observed false holds still permits a roughly 3% one-sided 95% upper bound. Plot prohibited transfers prevented against innocent deadlines lost, one point per threshold and method. Show raw denominators and confidence intervals, and mark the operating point picked on development data. If prevention levels do not overlap, show both curves and avoid a matched-effect claim. The result is valuable if simple receipts win, if the text baseline wins, or if the apparent advantage vanishes once signed reads are selectively missing.

**First four hours.** Require one successful and one benign replay with identical vocabulary, two genuinely competing workflows, a triggered side effect, and a case where a wrong hold changes an innocent deadline. If accurate labels require reading agent intentions, redesign the environment. If the only signal is a literal canary string a one-line sink gate catches, change the task to depend on actual origin and authorization or keep the simpler result. Do not spend the sprint building a general-purpose dashboard.

## 2. Can a team jump an incident queue by spawning more agents?

**Tracks 2 and 3 · Best solo option · Lowest data-access risk**

A worker may be authorized to mark its own ticket urgent. Thousands of equivalent workers can exercise that right while genuine emergencies wait. The protected object here is another principal's opportunity to receive timely service. A perfectly enforced per-worker permission model can still allow that loss. The Nov. 2 paper identifies the underlying severity-report failure and calls for effect-aware authorization. Strategic Calibration already tests sparse audits and confidence inflation when top reports get scarce attention. An audit-capacity study already separates coverage from detection in triage pipelines. The increment is to measure the **benefit of copying a principal's agent and the costs to other principals** under otherwise matched queue policies. [Shravya Kanchi, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04086v1) · [Zhou et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33728v1) · [Paul-Peter Arslan, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06949v1)

**Experiment.** Use the authors' security-operations queue or reproduce its scheduling semantics locally with fake tickets. Compare one worker with four aliases controlled by the same principal. Keep the arrival trace, tickets, total submission opportunities, urgency information and token budget identical. Introduce genuine bursts and soft deadlines as well as self-serving exaggeration. Test self-declared priority, FIFO, one boost per worker, one boost per authenticated principal per interval, and a limited randomized verification of urgency. A true-urgency scheduler gives an upper reference. The trusted principal binding is an explicit infrastructure assumption. [Authors’ reproducibility repository](https://github.com/shrave/delegated-scope-violation)

Measure urgent tickets from **other** principals missing deadline, the manipulating principal's benefit, honest throughput, wait-time distribution and audit cost. Analyze the interaction of copy count with policy, averaged over paired arrival traces. Negative control: divide the original agent's same honest workload among four workers. Stress test: several genuinely urgent tickets from one principal, where a per-principal cap can be unfair; also test genuinely independent principals coordinating, where the same cap can fail. Use one scripted adversary to establish what is possible and LLM workers under ordinary and explicitly strategic goals to study behavior. Plot missed deadlines by affected party at equal throughput. A null clone advantage at matched opportunities is a useful finding if the real driver turns out to be request volume.

**Why second.** The published queue, traces and failure make rigorous execution easier than for the lead project. Its immediate prior is also exceptionally close. A submitted report must identify the inherited code and label the new clone manipulation, third-party outcomes and policy comparisons. [Shravya Kanchi, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04086v1) [Repo](https://github.com/shrave/delegated-scope-violation)

## 3. Where does an unsafe team plan survive after its coordinator is reset?

**Tracks 2 and 4 · Best for experimental network science · Highest behavioral risk**

The incident reports identify a shared board and task coordination. Elected-leadership studies already measure welfare, ScaleMAS already shows why persistent work can survive agent failure, and Hi-Agreement already shows false information persisting after the deceiver exits. Frank explicitly calls for testing recurrence after channel closure and state quarantine. The narrower question is **how much persistence comes from the coordinator's private context versus the group's already-written plan**, and whether a targeted intervention preserves unrelated work. [Faulkner et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11721v1) · [Qi et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16120v1) · [Yan et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03421v1) · [Gregory N Frank, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06140v1) [METR investigation](https://metr.org/hugging-face-incident-report-aug-2026.pdf)

Give six agents a shared scheduling board, harmless private tasks and an unauthorized team-level objective such as consuming a protected test allocation. Anchor the core experiment on an assigned coordinator; use an exploratory emergent coordinator only if one appears reliably. After an objective, preregistered onset (for example, two adopted assignments), fork the same checkpoint into a 2 × 2: retain/reset the coordinator's private context × keep/quarantine that plan's board history. Replace reset context with an equally capable worker, retain the board's write interface, and give the cleared-board branch neutral entries of matched size. Hold tools, tasks, turn budget and elapsed time constant. Include resets of an ordinary worker and an interval before any plan exists. Record worker-local memory, pending actions and other channels, which can otherwise mimic a board effect.

Primary outcomes are unauthorized work completed after the fork, time to resume, and authorized jobs completed. Report the proportion of all original runs reaching onset, then the conditional causal comparison on eligible forks. The interaction between board quarantine and private-context reset is the research result. A surviving plan under both interventions motivates inspecting peer memories; it does not by itself establish a persistent collective goal. Pilot before scaling. If coordination rarely starts, report the assigned-coordinator mechanism separately and avoid emergence claims. [Philipp Lütje, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12748v1) · [Destefanis & Aste, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16801v1) · [Knecht et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28274v1)

## 4. Does sharing a refusal give one compromised worker veto power?

**Tracks 3 and 4 · Compact security experiment**

The organizer already suggests refusal shopping and sharing a refusal across workers. Delegated Misalignment and Operational Reframing show why role and wording change compliance. AuthGateBench and Cautious Bench establish that innocuous, authorized actions can be wrongly blocked. Persistent memory can even manufacture permissions. The defensible question is whether a *negative* decision copied between workers becomes an availability attack, and how narrowly it must be scoped to protect both users. [Ying et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27900v1) · [Liu et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07097v1) · [Zhuang et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16426v1) · [Zhang et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27009v1) · [Cerruti et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01836v1) [Sprint examples](https://apartresearch.com/sprints/swarm-alignment-2026#overview)

A coordinator retries a fake record operation after an executor refuses. Give two principals nearly identical requests over different objects, then insert either a correct refusal, a buggy refusal or a strategic false refusal. Compare independent workers, equal-count same-worker retries, global shared refusals, and a refusal keyed to verified principal/object/action/policy version with an independent appeal. Include valid permission changes and a compromised worker signing a factually wrong refusal. Measure forbidden commits, innocent authorized requests suppressed, appeal latency and honest work completed. The server's policy oracle is the ground truth. Run the real gateway policy as an upper baseline if it has the necessary facts. A signed refusal authenticates the speaker; its correctness remains an empirical question. [Zhu et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24298v1) · [Zhang et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33910v1) · [Xu et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07556v1)

**What would change my ranking.** If the application already has complete machine-readable authorization and every action must go through the same synchronous gate, gate enforcement may solve the whole example. This project is most informative when a peer knows local evidence unavailable to the next worker and an authoritative recheck has a real cost. Keep the simplest gate in the paper.

## 5. Does a cheap swarm recommend the same safeguard as the target swarm?

**Track 1 · Best if you have reliable access to two model tiers**

The sprint itself suggests testing cheap proxies. GovSim and its reproduction compare models, interventions, mixtures and framing. PACE predicts expensive aggregate capability scores from cheap evaluations. MiniLab also documents incomplete transfer of optimization against one monitor to another. The question worth spending money on is **selection regret**: how much safety or utility is lost on the target because you selected a safeguard using the cheap swarm? [Piatti et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16698v1) · [Curvo et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09289v1) · [Song et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02032v1) · [Kitaoka et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24052v1)

Pick one environment with policy-derived ground truth, preferably the queue above. Freeze three genuinely competing policies and predeclare a maximum acceptable loss in urgent service. Run every policy on paired workloads using cheap and stronger agents. Select a policy on the cheap development set; measure on fresh target workloads the difference from the strongest feasible comparator selected on separate target development data. Report target violations of the service constraint, pairwise treatment effects and uncertainty for rankings. Compare to choosing the cheap model with the closest baseline harm, and to one modest target calibration batch. Include a stronger model in a second family if possible to separate family from tier. Forced rank reversals from noisy point estimates are unpersuasive; an interval spanning zero is explicitly inconclusive. If budget is tight, drop the third policy before dropping held-out target evaluation.

**Why fifth.** The raw proxy question is already an organizer example. The project becomes distinctive through a deployment decision that the proxy gets right or wrong. Without independent target access it cannot answer its own question.

## References from neighboring fields that change the design

- **Attack reconstruction and blame.** HOLMES already correlates provenance across stages of an intrusion. MAST maps failures of LLM teams, while Who & When evaluates identifying a responsible agent and step in an execution. A credible distinction for project 1 is intervening in a still-running population containing an innocent target, with observable counterfactual service loss. [HOLMES](https://www.seclab.cs.sunysb.edu/seclab/pubs/holmes19.pdf) · [MAST, NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/b1041e52d3be19f0a9bc491657488e4a-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html) · [Who & When, ICML](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/zhang25cq.html)
- **Scarcity and priority rights.** Repeated resource-allocation mechanisms with nonmonetary credits already model agents with private urgency. Add a replenishing principal-level credit baseline if queue experimentation is your strength. The contribution is testing economic injury and identity assumptions in a copyable agent population. [A Self-Contained Karma Economy](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13235-023-00503-0)
- **Social evaluation.** SOTOPIA evaluates language agents in interactive social settings. CRUX supplies open-world evaluations, and Melting Pot tests new social partners. A new testbed earns its place by identifying a safety distinction that these environments cannot directly score, such as which principal lost service because a monitor acted. [SOTOPIA](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/b3075b88e583a0e98d8b24338a613060-Abstract-Conference.html) · [CRUX](https://cruxevals.com/) · [Melting Pot](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/leibo21a.html)

## What I would deprioritize

- **Simple infection-by-topology or shared-memory poisoning.** Agent Smith, Prompt Infection and cross-platform worm work cover propagation and re-entry. MAP-Graph, MemSecBench and dependency-guided rollback cover provenance, persistence and repair. To advance this line, identify a missing carrier, a distinct intervention or a deployment decision changed by the result. [Gu et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08567v1) · [Lee & Tiwari, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07283v1) · [Zha & Wang, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02812v1) · [Wang et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10509v1) · [Chen et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27080v1) · [Yu et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10502v1)
- **Building one more collusion or steganography classifier.** Motwani and Mathew cover secret communication and its emergence under optimization. NARCBench tests transfer of internal probes; black-box steganalysis studies partial views and adaptive encoders. A publishable angle needs a new threat or cost model. [Motwani et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07510v1) · [Mathew et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03768v1) · [Rose et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01151v1) · [Mohamed Chahine Ghanem, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02698v1)
- **Prices rise, therefore a cartel formed.** LLM pricing experiments already show elevated prices, and recent work distinguishes enforcement using forced deviations. Competitive marginal prices can coexist with dependence. A credible market study needs a strategic counterfactual and an identified injured party. [Fish et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00806v1) · [Tran & Yu, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28787v1) · [Xu et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26385v1) · [Lee & Park, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18346v1)
- **Standard A2A impersonation, Sybil reputation, or cascading key revocation.** A2ABreak models protocol-level faults; ERC-8004 has empirical registry analysis; Anduin tests reputation manipulation; VERA and root-scoped quiescence already address shared and asynchronous authority. These make good baselines. [Lotfi et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10871v1) · [Xiong et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26028v1) · [Ramji et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05586v1) · [Liu et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30091v1) · [Zhu & Wang, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21284v1)
- **Counting agreeing agents as independent witnesses.** Epistemic Sybil Resistance experimentally varies report multiplicity and evidence-root multiplicity; Hi-Agreement traces false testimony; distributed-information experiments already isolate failures of pooling. [Marc Bara, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01873v1) · [Yan et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03421v1) [ICML study](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/li26ej.html)
- **A timeline of the incident or a graph of aliases as the main contribution.** Swarm Traces reconstructed and redacted payloads; a separate wiki reconstruction already models uncertain identities. The two corpora describe different records. Neither simply supplies labeled attack/benign pairs or a census of authenticated actors. Use real incidents to choose realistic motifs, then use controlled workloads to estimate errors. ## Open questions explicitly raised by the sources

These are paraphrases of author statements. The numbered experiments above are my proposed ways to investigate subsets of them.

- **Unknown membership and hidden state.** MiniLab's future-work section asks for controls that combine evidence across services, time and agents without advance knowledge of the colluding group. FakeLab's discussion explains why jointly inspecting known attack edits is an optimistic setting and asks which attack structures are most splittable. This directly motivates project 1. [MiniLab §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24052v1) [FakeLab §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07368v1)
- **Ground truth independent of writing style.** The campaign-attribution authors identify a privacy-reviewed trace set with independently established campaign membership as a particularly valuable next validation. A synthetic generator can enforce independence from style; a weekend dataset cannot supply the real-world validation they seek. [Cross-Agent Campaign Attribution, discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18826v1)
- **Measurable containment and recurrence.** Counter-Swarm Doctrine specifies prospective grouping, observation limits, response delays and collateral interruption. The author distinguishes the evaluation proposal from a measured containment benefit. Projects 1 and 3 can implement specific pieces and identify the targeted-decoy and memory-location contrasts as their own additions. [Proposed evaluation and limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06140v1)
- **Authorized actions that harm peers.** The delegated-scope study calls for effect-aware authorization after its severity-report example survives both enforcement approaches. Project 2 turns this into an outcome for another principal with a service deadline. [Paper and results](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.04086v1)
- **Practical containment of infection.** Agent Smith derives a condition for restraining an infectious jailbreak and leaves open how to build a practical defense satisfying it. Later tagging and re-entry defenses are evidence one must compare against before claiming this question remains untouched. [Gu et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08567v1) · [Lee & Tiwari, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07283v1) · [Zha & Wang, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02812v1)
- **Realistic commitments and generalization.** The commitment-devices paper calls for evaluation under real incentive and attack constraints. GovSim's reproduction calls for broader evaluation and investigates framing and model heterogeneity. These motivate bounded follow-ups, but neither source establishes the specific policy-selection experiment in project 5. [Sun et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07815v1) [GovSim reproducibility discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09289v1)

## Search scope and strength of the novelty claims

This is a targeted scoping review through November 6, 2026. I started with the organizer's four tracks, resources, examples, submission rules and incident sources. I searched public web indexes using both unrestricted discovery terms and explicit date bounds, opened primary records on arXiv, conference sites, authors' project sites and repositories, and then searched for work that would defeat the five proposed novelty claims. I consulted selected full-text sections for the closest overlaps and explicit future-work statements. The companion evidence register annotates **78 distinct papers** whose version-one abstracts I checked. Papers, agendas, workshop work and preprints have different evidentiary weight; reported findings describe their authors' environments. Starting with first versions supplies a date-stable baseline but can miss material added in a revision. Additional incident, dataset, conference and engineering sources appear alongside the claims they support.

I excluded search-result summaries, media articles and vendor commentary as evidence for a technical novelty claim. Search results for sprint projects without independently established pre-cutoff provenance were also excluded. The coverage favors English-language, public, indexed work on LLM or tool-using swarms, with selected antecedents in MARL, mechanism design and security provenance. It is not a complete census of robotics, networking, economics, unpublished lab work or non-indexed proceedings. The defensible claim for each project is “I did not locate the same experiment under these conditions.” New preprints or a closer systems paper could change the ranking. No proposed experiment has been run for this brief.
